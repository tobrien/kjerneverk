## Feedback Given

> "The work that goes into coming up with a plan, or a prompt is something that you might need to recreate. Plans are not static documents they are 'trajectories of thought' <-- that sounds fucking awful but find a way to say that that's better."

## Resolution

Added "The Bigger Picture: Plans Are Not Documents" section:

- **Before creation**: Inception phase exists—contemplating the problem
- **After execution**: Plans don't freeze—they get referenced, adapted, revised
- **The insight**: Plans are "evolving records of reasoning—threads of thought that continue to develop, branch, and inform future work"

Replaced "trajectories of thought" with "evolving records of reasoning."

## Additional Feedback on Reproducibility

> "If you have a good plan, it's highly likely that you want to be able to apply a similar plan to another problem or project... Don't think about this as just apply a plan to another project, you sometimes want to apply the patterns of plan creation to the creation of another plan. This is a metacognitive approach to planning."

**Resolution**: Added aside on metacognitive planning:
> "Sometimes you want to apply the *patterns of plan creation* to creating another plan. How did we approach the problem? What questions did we ask? That's metacognition—thinking about how you think about planning."

## Key Insight

Plans aren't static documents moving from "not done" to "done." They're threads of thought that continue to evolve. The final version isn't "the answer"—it's where we stopped.
