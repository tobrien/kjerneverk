# Feedback: Language Corrections and Metacognition Emphasis

## Context
- **Date**: 2026-01-14 (morning)
- **Participants**: Tim (human), Claude (AI)
- **Platform**: Cursor IDE
- **Plan Version**: v0.7 → v0.7.1

## Feedback Given

### 1. "Human Suffering" Language Too Dramatic

> "This sentence is too dramatic: 'Technical requirements matter, but they emerge from human suffering.' I take exception to it because it's 'over the top' language. We live in a world full of human suffering, but plan creation isn't an example of that. Instead emphasize that it's a lot of really intense mental workload."

**Resolution**: Changed to:
> "The requirements in this document (R1 through R17) read like a feature checklist... This section answers that by describing what it's actually like to manage multiple AI-assisted projects across days and weeks. The features aren't abstract wants—they emerge from concrete frustrations."

### 2. Metacognition and Conversation Value

> "We're starting to get into metacognition, it's the line of reasoning (between a human and an LLM) that starts to set the parameters for what a plan ends up looking like, and there's so much value in that conversation that's also being lost. The big gap to identify is that we're losing value in the conversation."

**Resolution**: Added:
> "More critically: the conversation between human and AI is where the real work happens. It's metacognition—thinking about thinking, reasoning about approach, debating structure. That dialogue shapes what the plan becomes; the plan is just the crystallized output. When the conversation disappears, we lose the most valuable part of the process."

## Key Insight

The plan is the **artifact**; the conversation is the **value**. The document should emphasize that we're preserving metacognitive dialogue, not just tracking documents.
